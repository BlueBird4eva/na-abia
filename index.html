If you're lost right now

Stay where you are if it's a busy road, market, school gate or checkpoint — don't push further down an unfamiliar path, especially after dark.

  1. Call or flag down help at the nearest busy spot — a market, school, church or checkpoint is safer to approach than an empty road.
  2. If you have signal, call the numbers below. No signal? Ask a trader or okada rider to help place the call.
  3. Share your last known landmark — a junction, market or school name. Exact street addresses rarely exist here.
  4. Once safe, add the route to this guide so the next person doesn't repeat it.
📞 Call 112 — National Emergency Line

Opens your phone's SMS app with your current location pre-filled — you still tap send. Needs GPS + a moment of signal to attach your location; the message still opens without it, just without coordinates.

Ask for directions — common Igbo phrases

Useful when network fails and you need to ask someone on the road. Pronunciation varies by dialect across the Ngwa area, but these are widely understood.

Please, where is...?
Biko, ebee ka ... dị?
I am lost / I don't know my way
Efu m ụzọ
Please help me
Biko nyere m aka
Which way to Okpuala-Ngwa?
Kedu ụzọ e si aga Okpuala-Ngwa?
Thank you
Daalụ

Pronouncing village names

Every place has a "Hear it" button and a syllable guide (e.g. Say it: Ok·pu·a·la). The syllable split is generated automatically from Igbo spelling rules, so treat it as a rough guide rather than a tonal authority — Igbo is a tonal language and written syllables alone don't capture tone.

"Hear it" uses your phone's own text-to-speech. Most devices don't yet ship a native Igbo voice, so it will play in the closest voice available (usually English) rather than true Igbo pronunciation — still useful as a starting point, best paired with asking a local.

How this guide works

  • Verified — checked against public records: LGA headquarters, official secretariats, national emergency lines.
  • Community — added by app users. Useful, but not independently confirmed — double-check anything safety-critical.
  • Everything here works with no internet once open, including adding places and rating them — your changes save to this device. "Get directions" and the live map need a brief connection; with no signal, Map switches automatically to an offline schematic layout instead.
  • Your star ratings and notes on places are private to your device and never shared.

This guide covers all 17 LGAs of Abia State. Coverage is deepest for Isiala Ngwa North (HQ: Okpuala-Ngwa) and Isiala Ngwa South (HQ: Omoba) — the other 15 LGAs have a starting set of entries and will grow as more corpers and residents add verified places.
